Everything you need to build a resume that actually gets read.

Pick the guide that matches where you are. Professionals import and tailor an existing CV against a job description; Freshers build one from scratch with guided prompts. Both share the same Ask AI bar, templates, cover letters and publishing tools.

Getting Started

  1. Import your existing CVUpload a PDF, Word doc, or paste your text — Reeve extracts your experience, skills and education into an editable structure.
  2. Paste the job descriptionDrop in the JD for the role you're applying to so the tailoring and ATS scoring both work against the right target.
  3. Review the Quality ScoreCheck Vocabulary, Grammar, Text Errors, Structure and Completeness — each is clickable and jumps you straight to the issue.
  4. Tailor with the Ask AI barAsk it to add, rewrite or tighten anything in plain English — see the examples below.
  5. Export or publishDownload as PDF or Word, or publish a shareable link to send recruiters instead of an attachment.

FAQ

01How is the Professional module different from Fresher?+
Professional starts from a CV you already have and tailors it to a job description. Fresher starts from a blank profile and builds one up from courses, projects and skills with no work history assumed.
02How do I import my existing CV?+
Upload a PDF or Word file, or paste the text directly, when you start a new resume. Reeve parses it into sections automatically — review the result, since parsing an unusual layout can occasionally misplace a line.
03How does ATS scoring work, and what's a "good" score?+
The Structure sub-score checks whether your template's layout (columns, tables, text boxes) can be parsed correctly by screening software. 100% means the layout is safe; anything lower flags a specific risk and how to fix it.
04How do I tailor my resume to a specific job description?+
Paste the JD where prompted when you import or open a resume. Reeve highlights matching and missing keywords and skills so you can decide what to add — it won't invent experience you didn't have.
05What does the AI Builder change vs. what do I edit myself?+
The AI Builder only acts on what you ask it to, and only uses facts you've stated — it never invents numbers, employers or outcomes. Anything left blank after an AI add is shown clearly as blank, for you to fill in yourself.
06How do I ask the AI to add, update or delete something?+
Plain English works — you don't need a magic phrase. "Add skill: Kubernetes", "remove my second certification" or "explain my AI research work in a few lines under projects" are all understood, the same way you'd ask a person to update a document.
07Why didn't my change show up in the preview?+
The right-hand preview updates automatically a moment after any change. If it looks stale, try a hard refresh (Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+R) — your browser may be holding an older cached version of the page.
08Can I undo an AI change?+
Yes — every AI edit shows a confirmation of exactly what landed and where, with an undo option right there before you move on.
09How do I generate a cover letter from my resume?+
Open the Cover Letter tab, answer a few short prompts (company, role, tone) and it drafts a letter using your actual resume content — downloadable and saved separately under Cover Letters.
10How do I switch templates without losing my content?+
Templates only change layout and styling — your content stays exactly as it is. Click Templates in the left panel and pick any of the 25 templates at any time.

FAQ

01I have no work experience — what goes in my resume?+
Lead with Education, Projects, Courses and Skills. Internships, part-time work, volunteering and significant coursework all belong in Experience or Projects — recruiters reading graduate resumes expect this shape.
02How do I turn coursework or a project into resume content?+
Describe it to the Ask AI bar in plain language — what you built, studied or achieved — and tell it which section. It turns a rough description into proper resume-style lines without inventing details you didn't give it.
03How do I ask the AI to add a course, certification or skill?+
Just say it naturally — "add a certification for AIMS, Coursera, 2024" or "I completed a cybersecurity course with Google" both work, with or without the word "add".
04What's a good way to write my Summary with no job history?+
Fill in Education, Projects and Skills first, then ask the AI to write the summary — it draws on what's already there instead of generic filler, so fill in real content first.
05How does the Quality Score work for a fresher profile?+
The same five checks apply — Vocabulary, Grammar, Text Errors, Structure, Completeness — but Completeness is scored against what a graduate resume needs, not a 10-year career history.
06Can I add internships, volunteering or college projects as "experience"?+
Yes — anything with a role, a timeframe and real responsibilities belongs under Experience, whether or not it was a paid, full-time job.
07How do I add bullet points under a specific project or course?+
Name the project or course and what you want added — "add 2 bullet points about the results under my fraud detection project" — and it appends to that specific entry rather than creating a new one.
08Why does the AI sometimes put something under the wrong section?+
Name the section explicitly when a request could read more than one way — "...under projects" or "...in my summary" — and that explicit target always wins over a guess.
09How do I know if my resume will pass ATS screening with no work history?+
Check the Structure score — it evaluates your template's layout, not your experience level, so a graduate resume in a safe template scores exactly as well as a senior one.
10How do I publish a shareable link to my resume for recruiters?+
Open Publish from the preview panel, pick one of 7 visual templates, and get a clean, shareable link — no CV attachment needed.

Step-by-step guides

These work the same for Professionals and Freshers.

Saved Documents & published links
S1Where do I find my saved resumes?+
  1. Click Saved Documents in the left panel (or open your account menu, the round avatar top right, and choose Saved Documents).
  2. A window titled Saved opens with two tabs: Resumes and Cover Letters.
  3. Under Resumes, each saved resume is a row showing its name, template and last-updated date. Click Open to keep editing it.
S2Where exactly do I find my published link?+
  1. Open Saved Documents and stay on the Resumes tab.
  2. Find the resume you published and click Publish on its row.
  3. If that resume is already live, the window opens straight on your link (for example yoursite/cv/your-name) with Copy, View live and Unpublish buttons.
  4. If it isn't live yet, you'll see the publish form instead. Choose what to show, pick a template, then click Publish.
Each resume has its own link. You can have up to 10 published pages at a time.
S3How do I publish a resume step by step?+
  1. Save the resume first (the Save button in the toolbar). Only saved resumes can be published.
  2. Open Saved Documents → Resumes and click Publish on that resume's row.
  3. Under What's on the page, switch on any extras you want public: email, phone, photo. Experience, summary and skills are always shown.
  4. Type the ending of your link in the Link box. A green “Available” tick means it's yours.
  5. Choose one of the 7 looks, then click Publish.
  6. Copy the link and share it with recruiters. Use Unpublish any time and the link stops working immediately.
S4How many resumes can I save, and how do I keep one per job?+

You can save up to 100 resumes (and 20 cover letters). Use folders to keep one set per job description:

  1. Tailor your base resume to the job (see the guide below). The tailored copy is saved automatically into a folder named after the company (or the job title if you left the company blank).
  2. For any other resume, click Save, enter a name, then enter a folder name such as Barclays.
  3. Open Saved Documents → Resumes. Resumes are grouped under 📁 folder headings; click Open on any of them.
  4. To reorganise, click Move on a row and type a folder name (leave blank for no folder).
  5. Your original resume is never overwritten; each tailored version is a new resume.
Tip: name each resume clearly too, e.g. Senior GRC Manager - Oct 2026. Folders appear as soon as one resume uses that folder name.
S5Can I delete a saved resume or a published page?+
Yes. In Saved Documents click Delete on a row and confirm. Deleting is permanent. To take a published page offline without deleting the resume, open its Publish window and click Unpublish.
Tailor to a job description & ATS check
T1How do I tailor my resume to a job step by step?+
  1. Open or import the resume you want to start from.
  2. Click the gold Tailor your CV to Job Description button in the toolbar.
  3. Step 1 - Paste job: enter the job title, optionally the company, and paste the whole job listing. More text gives better results. Click Analyse & tailor.
  4. Step 2 - Review & apply: read your match score and the table of requirements. Tick the changes you want, edit any wording, or use Apply all verified.
  5. Click Apply changes, then Preview to see exactly where each change landed (amber highlights show what changed).
  6. Step 4 - Saved: give the tailored resume a name and click Save resume. It opens in the builder, ready to download.
T2How do I read the ATS match score?+
  1. The big ring is your Match Score: how many skills the job asks for are already evidenced in your resume.
  2. Matched: clearly present, nothing to do.
  3. Partial: you've done it but worded it differently from the job description, so reword it.
  4. Missing: related experience exists but this exact requirement isn't stated.
  5. Not found: nothing in your CV relates to it. This is a real gap, not a wording problem.
  6. Click the Matched, Partial or Missing tiles to jump to those rows.
The score estimates how you'd fare with screening software. It is not a promise of an interview.
T3Will tailoring invent experience I don't have?+
No. Reeve only reframes and reorders what is already true on your resume. Anything it can't verify is flagged and left for you to decide, and each suggested change is yours to accept or reject before anything is applied.
T4Why isn't my score 100%?+
Click Apply changes and a short summary explains what is still holding the score back, usually genuine gaps such as a tool or qualification you don't list. Those are not fixed by rewording. Add them only if they are true for you.
T5Can I tailor the same resume for many jobs?+
Yes. Each run saves a new resume under the name you choose, and your base resume stays untouched. Start the next job from the same base. Use Clear & Start New Job at the top of the Tailor window to wipe the previous job description first.
T6I closed the window by mistake. Is my pasted job lost?+
No. When you reopen Tailor you'll see “Welcome back - we restored your last pasted job and results”. Click Clear & Start New Job when you want a clean slate.
Cover letters
C1How do I create a cover letter step by step?+
  1. Open the resume you want the letter based on (the letter is drafted from the resume on screen).
  2. Click Cover Letter in the toolbar.
  3. Enter the Company and Job title.
  4. Pick a Tone: Formal, Confident (default) or Warm.
  5. Optionally add a note, such as why this company or a career change.
  6. Click Generate draft. The letter appears in an editable box.
  7. Edit any line, or click Regenerate for a fresh version.
C2How do I save or download a cover letter?+
  1. Click Save to keep it. It is stored under Saved Documents → Cover Letters.
  2. Use Word, PDF or HTML to download a copy.
  3. To come back later, open Saved Documents, switch to the Cover Letters tab and click Open.
You can keep up to 20 saved cover letters.
C3Will the cover letter make things up?+
No. It is drafted from the resume you have open, and nothing is added that isn't already on it. Use the optional note box if you want to mention something specific, and review the text before sending.
C4Can I write a different letter for each job?+
Yes. Change the company and job title, regenerate, and save. Give each saved letter a name that matches the job so you can tell them apart in the Cover Letters tab.
